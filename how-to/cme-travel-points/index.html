Paying for CME travel with points

Last reviewed 27 October 2026

This page contains my personal Chase referral link, and I am rewarded if you are approved. That is the only commercial arrangement on convene.md. It costs you nothing, it does not change the offer you receive, and no conference on this site has ever paid to be listed or ranked. If you would rather not use a referral link, go to Chase directly — the cards are the same.

Nothing here is financial advice. I am a surgeon, not an adviser, and whether any credit card makes sense depends on circumstances I know nothing about.

The problem is the timing, not the money

Most CME allowances reimburse. You book the flight in March for a September congress, the hotel wants a card at booking, and the money comes back weeks after you file the claim. For an international meeting that can be two or three thousand on your own card for half a year.

That spend is going on a card either way. The only question is whether it earns anything.

What a sign-up bonus is actually worth

The bonuses on travel cards are large enough to matter against a conference trip rather than just shaving a few per cent off it. Transferred to airline partners, a bonus in the 75,000–100,000 range is in the region of a long-haul return in economy, or several domestic flights — which is to say, roughly the travel cost of one overseas congress.

Offers change often, so treat any number you read here as indicative and check the current terms on the issuer's own page before applying. The figures below were advertised when this page was last reviewed.

When this is a bad idea

  • If you carry a balance. Interest on a revolving balance will exceed the value of any rewards programme, quickly and by a lot. Points are only worth chasing on spending you clear in full every month.
  • If your institution books travel directly. Plenty of departments book flights and hotels centrally. If the spend never touches your card, none of this applies.
  • If you are about to apply for a mortgage or refinance. A new account and the associated credit check can land at an unhelpful moment.
  • If the annual fee outruns the use. These are premium cards with real annual fees. They work if you travel enough to use the credits; they are poor value if the card sits in a drawer between conferences.

The card I use

I use a Chase Sapphire card for conference travel, which is why this is the one I can refer. As advertised at the time of writing: 100,000 bonus points with Sapphire Reserve®, or 75,000 bonus points with Sapphire Preferred®, subject to Chase's terms and spending requirements.
